Where was it going

When Howard Hertzog photographed USS Valley Forge from San Francisco Bay, she was a new ship — commissioned just months earlier into a Navy already reshaping itself for a world without the war it had been built to fight. The carrier's early life would be shaped not by the conflict she was built for, but by the one that would come after it.

Valley Forge departed for the Western Pacific as the world held its breath in the summer of 1950. When North Korean forces crossed the 38th parallel on June 25th, she was among the first U.S. Navy vessels to respond. On July 3rd, her aircraft flew the first carrier-based strikes of the Korean War against Pyongyang — making her a vessel whose story begins in one war's shadow and finds its defining moment in another's opening hours.

Howard's photograph captures her before any of that was known. She was a ship at the edge of what came next, passing through a bay full of ships doing the same thing, seen by a man who was simply there.
